POST-MORTEM REPORT: PM-2026-004
Date: 2026-07-11
Incident: DDoS Attack on Cairo-Fiber-East
Affected Node: Node 11 (Cairo-Fiber-East)

Timeline (UTC):
21:30 - Sudden spike in inbound traffic detected on Node 11's external transit links.
21:32 - Link utilization reached 100% (40 Gbps). Legitimate traffic began dropping due to interface congestion.
21:35 - NetFlow analysis identified a massive UDP flood targeting a specific customer IP block hosted behind Node 11.
21:40 - NOC applied BGP Flowspec rules to rate-limit UDP traffic destined for the targeted block.
21:55 - The attack morphed into a TCP SYN flood.
22:05 - Upstream blackholing (RTBH) was initiated for the specific targeted /32 IP addresses to save the broader network.
22:30 - Attack volume subsided.
23:15 - RTBH routing was removed, and traffic fully normalized.

Root Cause:
A distributed denial-of-service (DDoS) attack utilizing spoofed IP addresses targeted a gaming server hosted by an enterprise customer connected to Node 11. The attack saturated the transit links, causing collateral damage to other customers on the same node.

Resolution:
The attack was mitigated using a combination of rate limiting via BGP Flowspec and ultimately Remotely Triggered Black Hole (RTBH) routing to drop traffic to the specific targeted IPs at the network edge.

Impact:
General internet access for customers on Node 11 was severely degraded for about 35 minutes. The specific targeted customer was offline for approximately 100 minutes due to the blackholing.

Lessons Learned:
1. We need to implement an automated DDoS mitigation appliance (e.g., Arbor Networks) at the network edge to detect and scrub malicious traffic automatically before it saturates internal links.
2. Customer communication during security events needs to be more streamlined.
